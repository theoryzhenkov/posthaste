## Top-10 ranking

1. **S1 — Duplicate email send on timeout** (`outbox.rs:31-34,492-501` + 10 s timeout): user-visible, third-party-visible, plausibly frequent on slow servers. Critical.
2. **A1 — OAuth refresh race + last-writer-wins rotation overwrite** → permanent `invalid_grant` lockout requiring manual re-auth. High.
3. **PP1 — Silent push death** (no keepalive/read-deadline, status lies, dead WS keeps eating mutations at 10 s each). High.
4. **F2 — 10 s total-request timeout monoculture**: large blobs and slow-link syncs fail deterministically forever. High.
5. **PP2 — Reconnect counter-reset defect**: accept-then-drop servers loop at 5 s forever, WS→SSE fallback unreachable, jitterless herd. (Lifecycle-debt row 4 confirmed and expanded.) High.
6. **PP3 — No catch-up sync on push reconnect + WS `pushState` resume unused**: guaranteed notification-loss windows up to the 60 s poll. Medium.
7. **S2 — `replace_all` snapshot clobbers unsettled optimistic writes** (acknowledged TODO(S3)): visible UI reverts under `cannotCalculateChanges` fallback. Medium.
8. **S3 — Full snapshot has no partial checkpoint**: flaky link + big mailbox = endless O(N) refetch, initial sync may never complete. Medium.
9. **F1/F3 — No retry policy and failure-class collapse to `Network(String)`**: no 429/`Retry-After` handling, no differentiated handling of DNS/TLS/5xx. Medium.
10. **P1 — Empty-string ids from `unwrap_or_default()`** on mailbox/email/thread ids: malformed server data corrupts store keying. Medium.

Honorable mentions: A2 (tick swallows `invalid_grant`), C1 (single-loop starvation), P3 (positional response trust in send — a feeder for S1).

## Verdict

The subsystem is architecturally mature but operationally naive: the layering (ports in `posthaste-domain-service`, thin gateway impls, hint-only push, trigger coalescing) and the persistence story (single-transaction batch+cursor commits, withheld-cursor streaming, idempotent re-sync — S0) are genuinely strong, clearly benefiting from the same design discipline as the link stack. But everything at the network edge is first-draft: not a single retry, jitter, rate-limit, or keepalive exists in the engine; one 10-second timeout governs everything from a keyword flip to a 20 MB blob; the reconnect loop has a confirmed logic defect that neutralizes both its backoff and its transport fallback; token refresh has no concurrency control over a rotating credential; and the one place where at-most-once actually matters (send) retries a non-idempotent operation. Where the link stack has an explicit reconnect engine, resume-from-`after_seq` semantics, and a tracked lifecycle-debt ledger (`R/posthaste-authority-server/src/local_authority_server.rs:492,531`), the provider engine's only systemic safety net is the 60-second poll tick — which papers over push death, reconnect loss, and rate limiting alike while hiding all of them from observability. Relative to the link stack this subsystem is roughly one refactor generation behind: correct-by-construction at rest, fragile in motion.
